# The Tragic Fate of the Alsberg Family – The Human Costs of "Aryanization" and the Role of Helmut Horten

The story of the Alsberg family and their once flourishing department store empire is a harrowing testimony to the crimes of National Socialism and the systematic destruction of Jewish life and entrepreneurship in Germany. It illustrates exemplarily the foundation of injustice and human suffering upon which the wealth of some profiteers of the Nazi era, such as Helmut Horten, was built. This article sheds light on individual fates, economic expropriation, and the profound effects that National Socialist ideology had on the lives of these German citizens.

## The Roots of a Department Store Giant: The Alsberg Family and "Gebrüder Alsberg AG"

The family history of the Alsbergs, inextricably linked with the rise of the modern department store in Germany, begins with **Salomon Alsberg**, a merchant who was the father of eleven children. One of his children, **Siegfried Alsberg (1850–1935)**, was to become the visionary founder and motor of one of the most significant department store groups in the German Reich: "Gebrüder Alsberg AG", headquartered in Cologne.

Under Siegfried Alsberg's leadership and with the participation of his wife **Emma Alsberg (née Hess, 1857–1942)** and other family members, the company developed into a flourishing empire. The Alsberg department stores were known for their quality, broad range, and innovative business practices. They shaped the city centers of numerous German metropolises and medium-sized towns, including:

*   **Bochum:** With the imposing "Kortumhaus" on Hochstraße (now Kortumstraße), built between 1913 and 1921, the first large department store in the city.
*   **Duisburg:** A flagship store at the corner of Beekstraße / Universitätsstraße.
*   **Neuss:** A central point for textiles and fashion at Oberstraße 91.
*   **Gelsenkirchen:** At Bahnhofstraße 53 (now WEKA-Karree) and Gelsenkirchen-Buer.
*   **Dresden:** At Wilsdruffer Straße, König-Albert-Passage, and Schloßstraße.
*   **Other locations:** Osnabrück, Recklinghausen, Witten, Bielefeld, Detmold, Hagen, Hamm, Hildesheim, Iserlohn, Kassel, Koblenz, Lüdenscheid, Mülheim an der Ruhr, Oberhausen, Oldenburg, Remscheid, Solingen, Wanne-Eickel, Wattenscheid, and Wuppertal-Elberfeld.

This presence made "Gebrüder Alsberg AG" a cornerstone of German retail and the Alsberg family a respected entity in the economic middle class.

## "Aryanization": Systematic Expropriation and Destruction of Jewish Life

With the Nazi seizure of power in January 1933, an unprecedented process of persecution, discrimination, and systematic expropriation of Jewish citizens began, euphemistically termed "Aryanization." Jewish businesspeople were specifically pressured to sell their companies far below value or forced to give them up entirely. This was not merely an economic act, but a brutal assault on the foundations of existence and the dignity of Jewish families.

### The Case Study of Duisburg and the Role of Helmut Horten

A particularly prominent and historically significant example of "Aryanization" concerns the Alsberg department store in **Duisburg**. On **May 1, 1938**, the then only 27-year-old **Helmut Horten** took over this significant store of the Alsberg chain. This transaction was no ordinary business takeover under fair market conditions. It took place in the context of massive state pressure, boycott measures, and the life-threatening situation of the Jewish owners. Horten, who profited from this policy, laid the foundation for his later department store empire with this and similar "Aryanizations." His wealth is thus inextricably linked with the injustice and suffering inflicted on the Alsberg family and countless other Jewish entrepreneurs in the course of "Aryanization."

## Individual Fates: The Human Tragedy of the Alsberg Family

The personal suffering of the Alsberg family went far beyond the loss of their life's work. It is a story of expulsion, degradation, deportation, and murder that reveals the full extent of National Socialist crimes.

### Siegfried and Emma Alsberg: The Founder Generation

**Siegfried Alsberg** passed away in 1935, before the most brutal phases of persecution. His widow, **Emma Alsberg (née Hess)**, born on November 29, 1857, in Kempen, witnessed the escalation of terror. Her last freely chosen residence was at Stadtwaldgürtel 43 in Cologne. From there, she was deported to the Theresienstadt ghetto on June 15, 1942. The conditions in Theresienstadt, which served as a "model ghetto" for Nazi propaganda but was in truth a transit and death camp, were inhumane. Emma Alsberg died there on December 1, 1942, at the age of 85 from exhaustion and the effects of imprisonment.

### Dr. Alfred Alsberg and Martha Alsberg: The End of a Family

Their son, the doctorate lawyer and World War I officer **Dr. Alfred Alsberg (born 1883)**, was not only a co-owner but also director of Gebrüder Alsberg AG and managed, among others, the store in Bochum (Kortumhaus). Even after the Nazi takeover, he was initially tolerated as a managing director because his expertise was essential for the operation. But the pressure grew relentlessly. The Kortumhaus was forcibly renamed "Kaufhaus Kortum" in 1933/1935 to erase the Jewish name.

Alfred Alsberg and his wife **Martha Alsberg (née Eichengrün, 1895–1944)** lived last at Morsdorfer Hof 35 in Cologne. In October 1941, they were deported from there to the Litzmannstadt (Łódź) ghetto. Catastrophic conditions prevailed in this ghetto. **Alfred Alsberg** starved to death there on November 14, 1943. **Martha Alsberg** was taken to the Kulmhof (Chełmno) extermination camp after her deportation from Litzmannstadt in 1944 and murdered there. Her death in the extermination camp is further evidence of the systematic genocide by the National Socialists.

### The Surviving Children of Alfred and Martha: An Escape into the Unknown

Amidst this immeasurable suffering, there were also stories of survival, often through heroic efforts or lucky circumstances. Three children of Alfred and Martha Alsberg were able to escape the Holocaust:

*   **Eva Alsberg (born 1924)** succeeded in escaping with a Kindertransport to England. She later married the diplomat Herbert Samuel Walker. Her fate is immortalized by a Stolperstein (stumbling stone) in front of her former school, the Königin-Luise-Schule in Cologne.
*   Her brothers **Fritz Alsberg (1920–2009)** and **Heinz Alsberg (1921–2008)** also managed to flee abroad in time and thus escape certain death.

### The Josephs Family in Neuss: An Extinguished Generation

The tragedy of the Alsbergs was not limited only to the direct founding family. Closely connected with the company was the **Josephs family** in Neuss. The manager of the Alsberg department store in Neuss, **Gustav Josephs (born 1881)**, was a defining figure of local economic life. After the "Aryanization" of the business in 1939, he fled with his family to the Netherlands. But they were not safe there from the German occupation either. The family was interned in the Westerbork transit camp and deported from there to Auschwitz.

*   **Gustav Josephs** was murdered in Auschwitz on September 17, 1942.
*   His wife **Käthe Josephs (née Felsenthal, 1890)** shared his fate and was also murdered in Auschwitz on September 17, 1942.
*   Their three daughters were also murdered in Auschwitz:
    *   **Ruth Josephs (born 1921)** on September 17, 1942.
    *   **Lotte Josephs (born 1917)** on September 30, 1942.
    *   **Ilse Josephs (born 1919)** on September 30, 1942.

The entire nuclear Josephs family was extinguished by the Holocaust – a staggering example of the regime's intent to annihilate. Their story is preserved in Neuss by five Stolpersteine in front of their last freely chosen home at Loerickstraße 6, whose sponsorship was taken over by students from the Marienberg Gymnasium.

### Other Victims from the Extended Circle

Other relatives of the Alsbergs also fell victim to the racial mania. **Laura Johanna Alsberg (née Oppenheim, 1861–1943)**, the wife of Rudolf Alsberg (a relative of Siegfried), lived last at Lindenthalgürtel 43 in Cologne. She was also deported to the Theresienstadt ghetto and murdered there. Her fate is likewise honored by a Stolperstein in Cologne.

Two further Stolpersteine in Neuss commemorate **Albert Joseph (born 1862)** and **Julie Joseph (born 1866)** at Drususallee 81. They too were deported to Theresienstadt on July 22, 1942. Albert Joseph died there on September 27, 1942, Julie Joseph on January 9, 1943. Although the direct connection of these Josephs to Gustav Josephs is not explicit, their fates testify to the broad terror against the Jewish population.

## Remembrance, Warning, and Processing History

The memory of the victims of the Alsberg family and all those who had to suffer under National Socialist tyranny is of immense importance. **Stolpersteine**, small brass plaques in the pavement in front of the last freely chosen homes of the victims, remind us today in Cologne, Gelsenkirchen, and Neuss of the lives and murders of these people.

Their names are also immortalized in the databases of Holocaust memorials, such as the **Yad Vashem** database of the names of Shoah victims. Local initiatives, such as the research by students of the Königin-Luise-Schule in Cologne, actively contribute to preserving these stories from oblivion.

The story of the Alsberg family is a powerful reminder that the wealth acquired through "Aryanization" is based on coercion, suffering, and murder. It warns us to understand the mechanisms of such crimes, to critically question the role of profiteers, and to actively oppose anti-Semitism, racism, and any form of discrimination. The continued engagement with the history of the Alsbergs is an important contribution to the culture of remembrance and to shaping a responsible future.
