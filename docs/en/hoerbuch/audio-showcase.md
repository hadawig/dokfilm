They don't understand pointillism. They look through a magnifying glass at an individual amount and scream 'scandal!'. But step back for a moment. Look at the whole picture. Isn't it magnificent? Isn't it stable? The silence we afford ourselves is the most expensive art form in the world. And I am its greatest patron. When I leave, the money doesn't remain. The glow remains. "I have many names in this project. I am the curator who counts the paintings as the shadows grow longer. I am Alfred Alsberg, the man whose name was removed from history with sandpaper. And I am the chronicler who writes it all down while the world outside pretends nothing happened.

The history of money is a history of deletion. If you walk through Duisburg today, or through Gelsenkirchen, search for traces of my family. You won't find them. They are buried under layers of concrete and glossy advertisements. Helmut Horten was a master of the surface. He knew that people don't want to look deep as long as the shop window is brightly lit.

But memory... memory is like water. You can try to dam it, you can try to channel it, but in the end, it always finds its way to the surface. The 49,000 euros haunting the headlines today are just the modern tears of a system that already began to cry in 1936.

We're not staging a drama here. We're staging an inventory. A taking stock of the silence. Every scene, every word in this screenplay is an attempt to make the echo of the past audible in the hustle and bustle of the present. Listen closely. The clicking of the registers, the whispering in the back rooms, the breathing of those who have lost everything. It's all there. You just have to have the courage to turn off the music of gold for a moment to endure the silence behind it."
